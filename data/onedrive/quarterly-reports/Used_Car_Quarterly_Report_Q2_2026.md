# moby-cars Used-Vehicle Quarterly Report — Q2 2026

*Prepared by Used-Vehicle Analytics. All figures are fictional demo data.*

Sources: `Used_Vehicle_Sales_Q2_2026.csv`, `Wholesale_Price_Index_Q2_2026.csv`, `Days_To_Turn_By_Segment_Q2_2026.csv`,
`Auto_Loan_Performance_Q2_2026.csv`.

## Headlines

- **Volume:** 612,000 used vehicles sold in Q2, building every month: 195,590 in April, 200,370 in May and 216,040 in June.
- **Retail price:** the unit-weighted average retail price was **$28,938**.
- **Wholesale:** the composite wholesale index fell **1.3%** over the quarter (close of 2026-03-28 to close of 2026-06-27),
  led by electric vehicles at **−5.2%**.
- **Turn time:** the average vehicle took **43.7 days** to sell; 11.7% of stock has been on the lot more than 60 days.
- **Credit:** 30+ day delinquency on the auto-loan book is **3.90%** (60+: 1.60%), concentrated in the 2025 cohorts written
  at peak prices.

## Pricing

Wholesale softened in every segment. The electric index fell from 156.2 to 148.1 and is **14.8% below a year ago**; its
average auction price closed the quarter at $22,510. SUVs (−0.8%) and trucks (−0.4%) held up best. Retail prices are
falling more slowly than wholesale, so the retail-to-auction gap is widest for electric vehicles ($7,190) and SUVs
($6,190).

## Inventory and turn time

Sedans (52 days) and electric vehicles (58 days) are the slow segments. The single slowest market is **sedans in the
Midwest at 61 days**, followed by electric vehicles in the Southwest (60.7) and the Northeast (60.1). 19% of electric
stock is older than 60 days, against 7% for trucks.

| Prime | 1.41% | 0.46% |
| Near prime | 4.62% | 1.85% |

The 2025-Q1 and 2025-Q2 origination cohorts, written when used prices peaked, run at about **6.2%** 30+ delinquency,
against 1.1% for loans written this quarter. Near-prime and subprime loans from 2025-Q2 and subprime loans from 2025-Q3
now carry a current loan-to-value above 100%: falling wholesale prices leave the vehicle worth less than the loan.

## Outlook and watch list

1. Electric-vehicle pricing: a further wholesale decline would push more EV stock past 60 days.
2. Midwest sedans: the slowest market; consider pricing action.
3. 2025 peak-price cohorts: rising delinquency with negative equity.
